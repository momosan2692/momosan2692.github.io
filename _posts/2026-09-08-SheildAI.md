## 2. Hivemind Software Architecture

Three-layer structure:
1. **Hivemind Edge** — on-vehicle "brain"; platform-agnostic, modular, built for GPS/comms-denied operation.
2. **Hivemind Design** — dev tooling for building/testing autonomy behaviors.
3. **Hivemind Commander** — human-in/on-the-loop supervisory interface.

**EdgeOS** (real-time middleware, *not a kernel*):
- Organized around **Blocks** (modular compute units) and **Ports** (typed I/O, sync/async messaging).
- Benchmarked sustaining millions of messages/sec, low latency.
- **Static JSON configuration at launch** — no dynamic node creation at runtime (unlike ROS). Deliberate design choice for auditability/certification.
- Local shared-memory message passing (sub-millisecond latency).
- **EOSLang** — proprietary schema/messaging toolchain (C++ optional/variant types, Eigen integration, multi-format serialization, Python/TypeScript bindings).
- **Communicator Library** — multi-agent discovery, time sync, QoS, network-condition feedback.

### Underlying OS/hardware (not disclosed per-platform)
Shield AI does not design its own RTOS or chips. Per job postings, engineers work with:
- **RTOS candidates:** VxWorks (Wind River), RTLinux, Green Hills Integrity — industry-standard certifiable options for flight-critical control.
- **Nvidia Jetson** (Jetson Linux / L4T) — likely used for GPU-heavy perception/AI inference, not hard-real-time control.
- Likely a **mixed-criticality architecture**: certified RTOS for flight control, Jetson Linux for perception, EdgeOS as the abstraction layer above both.
- **Exact combination per aircraft (V-BAT, X-BAT, etc.) is not publicly disclosed.**

## 4. Certification / Validation Pathway (General, Not Taiwan-Specific)

1. **A-GRA (Autonomy Government Reference Architecture)** — Air Force open standard (MOSA-based) decoupling autonomy software from airframe hardware. Cross-vendor tests (Shield AI on Anduril's Fury; Collins on GA's YFQ-42A) exist specifically to prove no vendor lock-in.
2. **DO-178C** — airborne software safety standard (5 Design Assurance Levels, A–E). Central challenge for AI/ML: proving **determinism** and **full structural code coverage** — a poor natural fit for learned/adaptive behavior, which is why EdgeOS's static-config design matters.
3. **DO-254** — hardware equivalent (FPGAs/ASICs).
4. **Known friction point:** modular/open architectures (MOSA, FACE) don't exempt a system from full recertification on minor changes — certification cycles (years) lag innovation cycles (months).
5. Military variants often substitute **Mission Success Probability (MSP)** for non-safety-critical mission/autonomy logic, reserving strict DO-178C rigor for actual flight-control code.

**No unified, public "AI autonomy certification checklist" exists yet** — it's a patchwork of an interoperability standard (A-GRA) layered on decades-old deterministic-software safety standards.

## 5. Rules of Engagement / Human Control (X-BAT, as stated by Shield AI executives)

- **Stated doctrine:** human "on the loop" (monitors, can intervene) at minimum for offensive kill decisions — not necessarily "in the loop" (approves every individual shot). Terminology used inconsistently across Shield AI's own statements.
- **Concrete scenario (SVP Armor Harris):** X-BAT assigned a "box of airspace" to patrol. Within that box, onboard IFF (friend-or-foe) checks run automatically; a target failing ID can be **automatically engaged per pre-set rules of engagement**, without per-target human sign-off. Compared explicitly to the Navy's Phalanx CIWS.
- **Co-founder Brandon Tseng:** autonomous systems should not make moral decisions about lethal force — describes this as current US/NATO policy (DoD Directive 3000.09: "appropriate levels of human judgment").
- **Net effect:** human authorizes the *policy/rules of engagement* in advance (human-in-the-loop at that stage); the system executes detect→identify→engage within that envelope at machine speed (human-on-the-loop at the trigger moment).
- **Open/unresolved (flagged by outside analysts, not Shield AI):** authorization requirements during comms loss (relevant since Hivemind targets comms-denied ops), behavior in contested/complex airspace, responsibility attribution for unintended engagements.
- **No public technical spec** of the actual decision-architecture implementation — only executive interview statements and marketing-level middleware detail.

- **Context:** This single order (280) exceeds Shield AI's entire global V-BAT deliveries through late 2024 (~250 units).
- **Purpose:** give Taiwan's surface warfare groups and littoral units organic, runway-independent ISR/targeting capability, reducing reliance on shore-based radar and fixed airfields (vulnerable in a contested Strait scenario). V-BAT's GPS-denied visual-odometry navigation is a direct fit for a jammed-EW environment.
- **Parallel domestic program:** Taiwan is separately ordering 32 units of its own NCSIST **Albatross II** drone (NT$16.78B) — a two-tier strategy: mass-volume foreign V-BATs + smaller higher-end domestic drones.

### "How many Hivemind units in this order?"
Hivemind is not sold/counted as a standalone SKU — it's embedded per airframe.
- **~280** = Hivemind Edge instances (one per aircraft, onboard autonomy)
- **~140** = Hivemind Commander instances (one per ground control station, human-supervision layer)
- No public source itemizes a separate "software license count."

### Sea-domain tie-in
- **Thunder Tiger (雷虎科技) + Shield AI MOU** (May 2026): integrates Hivemind autonomy onto Thunder Tiger's unmanned surface vessels (USVs). Demonstrated an AI-powered attack-boat fleet at Dapeng Bay, Pingtung, including simultaneous control of 6 USVs from one station.
- This makes Taiwan one of the most Hivemind-saturated militaries outside the US — Hivemind-powered air (V-BAT) and sea (Thunder Tiger USVs) simultaneously.

## 9. Thunder Tiger (雷虎科技) "CHN Chip" Controversy

**Note: this is a separate, smaller Army procurement — not the V-BAT or USV deal with Shield AI — but it raises supply-chain trust questions relevant to the larger contracts.**

- **The case:** Thunder Tiger won an Army contract (~NT$20+ million) for "training suicide drones" (10th Corps) and "immersive training drones" (6th Corps).
- **May 13, 2026:** During first-stage acceptance inspection, Army logistics personnel visually spotted a flight-control chip marked "CHN" (some reports say the marking appeared partially ground/polished off). Acceptance was suspended per regulation; investigation opened.
- **Thunder Tiger's response:** The chip is manufactured by **STMicroelectronics** (French/European), with a Certificate of Origin listing France. Company argues "CHN" is merely a **back-end packaging/testing location code**, not the legal country of origin — citing WTO practice and Taiwan MOEA standards, which define origin by where "substantial transformation" (core IP design, wafer fabrication) occurs, not packaging.
- Thunder Tiger has submitted full supply-chain traceability documentation and Certificates of Origin to the procurement authority, denies using Chinese components, and is suing the media outlet that reported the story for defamation/stock-price damage (NT$50 million civil claim + criminal complaint under the Securities and Exchange Act).
- **Outside criticism:** some commentators note the issue was only caught because an inspector visually noticed a (allegedly) incompletely-erased marking — raising the question of whether Taiwan's "non-Red supply chain" verification is a systematic, proactive audit or a reactive, visual spot-check.
- **Counterpoint / credibility context:** Thunder Tiger is the first Asian drone manufacturer to receive the US DoD's **Blue UAS** cybersecurity certification (software/hardware traceability to US military-grade standards), and its Overkill FPV suicide-drone line advanced to Stage 2 of the US DoD's Blue UAS evaluation, winning a ~1,520-unit (~US$7.6M) US Stage-1 procurement order. This suggests Thunder Tiger's rigor is inconsistent across sales channels — stronger scrutiny on the US-facing track than apparently applied on this specific Taiwan Army contract.

### Relevance to the Shield AI V-BAT / Hivemind order
- No public evidence ties this chip controversy to Hivemind software or to the Shield AI V-BAT/USV deals specifically — the disputed contract is a separate, smaller Army drone procurement.
- However, it highlights a **structural verification gap**: if hardware-origin checks on a small contract can hinge on visual inspection rather than systematic auditing, the much larger and more strategically sensitive V-BAT/Hivemind deal raises a fair question about whether comparably rigorous, independent verification exists for a system involving proprietary US autonomy software.

## 10. Verification / Transparency Outlook (Assessment, Not Confirmed Fact)

A reasonable expectation, based on industry norms rather than any Shield AI statement:

- **ITAR/EAR export controls** legally restrict how much of Hivemind's source code or decision logic can be shared with any foreign customer, including allied governments — this is a legal floor, not just a business preference.
- **Standard defense-export practice** (seen with F-16 sales, etc.) delivers a "black box" system: customers get operational interfaces and limited maintenance access, not source-code-level transparency.
- **Realistic verification available to a customer like Taiwan** is likely limited to:
  - Flight test data (behavioral/black-box validation)
  - Third-party certifications (e.g., Blue UAS-style, US-government-backed)
  - Contractual performance guarantees and liability terms
  - Interface-level integration testing (confirms Hivemind talks correctly to Taiwan's radar/C4ISR systems — does not validate internal decision logic)
- This mirrors the unresolved industry-wide tension already noted in Section 5: regulators and customers want assurance that AI decision-making is safe and ROE-compliant, but suppliers cannot legally or commercially expose the internals needed to fully verify that independently.
