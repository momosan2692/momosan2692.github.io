Anduril and Taiwan

A video transcript about Palmer Luckey, his defense company Anduril, and the systems Taiwan has bought, may buy, or probably should skip. Each claim was checked against current reporting. Most numbers hold up. Several details are outdated, mixed up, or unsupported, and they are marked below.

What the checking found

  • Confirmed: Anduril is valued at $61B after a $5B round in May 2026, and Taiwan signed a NT$26.9B (about $847M) contract in August 2026 for 1,554 Altius-700M and 478 Altius-600ISR drones.
  • Updated: The video says Taiwan does not have the Barracuda-500 yet. Taiwan's NCSIST has since shown a Taiwan version of it and says the whole supply chain will be local.
  • Corrected: The anti-drone quadcopter is Anvil, not Pulsar (Pulsar is electronic warfare). Kuwait has a US-approved possible sale, not a completed purchase. Copperhead is a torpedo-type underwater vehicle, not a seabed missile that flies out of the water.
  • Not verified: The Pittsburgh billboard location, the "$17 million startup" figure, the Roadrunner rumor for Taiwan, and the claim that a car factory could become a missile factory in a week.
The founder

From Oculus to a defense company

Luckey founded Oculus VR in April 2012 and ran a Kickstarter that raised $2.4 million. Facebook later bought the company; the reported price is about $2 billion, and Forbes estimated Luckey's net worth at $700 million in 2015.

In September 2016 he gave $10,000 to Nimble America, a pro-Trump group whose billboard showed Hillary Clinton under the caption "Too Big to Jail." Some game developers paused support for Oculus and Luckey apologized. He left Facebook in March 2017. Neither side explained it then, and Mark Zuckerberg told the Senate in 2018 that it was not over a political view. The Wall Street Journal reported in 2018 that executives pressured him to publicly back a different candidate and that he negotiated a payout of at least $100 million. Facebook denied that politics was the reason.

Anduril was incorporated on April 20, 2017 (some accounts give June 2017) with five founders in total: Luckey, Trae Stephens, Matt Grimm, Joe Chen and Brian Schimpf, who later became CEO. The name is Aragorn's sword in The Lord of the Rings.

The pitch

Build the product first, then sell it

The video's argument is that traditional contractors wait for the military to define a need, then build on a cost-plus basis with little reason to come in under budget. Anduril instead guesses the need, builds the system, and offers it. That is the company's stated model, and the critique of cost-plus contracting is a widely held view, but nothing I found measures how much it saves.

The claim that matters most for Taiwan is manufacturability. For the Barracuda, Anduril says it takes 50% less time to produce, needs 95% fewer tools and 50% fewer parts, and costs 30% less than unnamed competitors. Anduril also says assembly needs ten or fewer tools and can use automotive and consumer-electronics workers. A trade analyst reports assembly in as little as 30 hours and about 70% commercial content. No independent body has verified any of this. The video's picture of a scooter factory becoming a cruise-missile factory "within a week" goes beyond what these sources say. Anduril's new Arsenal-1 plant in Ohio, a roughly $1 billion project, will build Barracuda missiles, Roadrunner and the Fury jet.

Taiwan

The relationship so far

Luckey spoke on September 19, 2026 at the Resilient Taiwan for Sustainable Democracy forum, hosted by the Presidential Office at the Grand Hyatt Taipei. He argued that freedom is Taiwan's strongest asset, that Taiwan needs asymmetric methods because China spends roughly 8 to 10 times more on defense, and that its machine-tool, semiconductor and component industries could build weapons in the millions. He said Taiwan should export defense products so other countries depend on them the way they depend on TSMC. The video says the visit was "about a week ago," so it was probably recorded in late September. China sanctioned Anduril and its executives in July 2024 over arms sales related to Taiwan.

Category 1 · Bought

Altius, Dive-LD

The Altius is a tube-launched loitering munition: it is launched, flies until it finds a target, then strikes. Taiwan has three versions on order or in service.

VersionWarheadRangeEnduranceRole
Altius-600M4 kg~160 km~1.5 hLoitering munition
Altius-700M~15 kg~160 km~1.25 hLoitering munition, aimed at armor and air defense
Altius-600ISRnonen/a~3.5 hSurveillance, target designation, electronic warfare

The video calls the June test a 100% hit rate. That figure comes from the military's own footage of the Tienma Exercise, where the drones fired more than a dozen rounds at maritime targets. It sits next to a February report that only two of five drones completed an earlier live-fire test, which the Army disputed. A supply issue was also reported: the original chargers were made in China and were not covered by the US sale, so US technicians had to come to Taiwan to charge the drones. The military and the US have since developed a non-China battery and charger chain, with batteries to be built in Taiwan.

Dive-LD

The video says Taiwan has bought the Dive-LD, an unmanned submarine with no attack role. That is right with limits: NCSIST acquired a single vehicle, with delivery planned for August 2026, and for now it is restricted to civilian work by US export controls and agreements with Anduril. The first use is inspecting undersea cables with Chunghwa Telecom. Anduril lists it at 5.8 m long, 1.2 m in diameter, rated to 6,000 m and up to 10 days submerged. NCSIST wants to adapt it with a multibeam sonar to look for mines and submarines.

Category 2 · Probably next

Barracuda, Lattice, Copperhead

Barracuda cruise missiles

The family has three sizes. The video focuses on the 500. Its range is given as 800 km in the video and on Wikipedia, and as more than 500 nautical miles (926 km) in an Anduril-sourced profile, so treat the exact number as approximate. The 45 kg figure is the payload capacity, not a published warhead. Unit costs are scattered because the sources measure different things.

Does Taiwan have it? The video says no. That is out of date. In September 2025 NCSIST showed a land-mobile missile based on the Barracuda-500, said the whole supply chain would be in Taiwan, and planned production within 18 months. Anduril has also shown ground launch, and the US Air Force's pallet-launch route (Rapid Dragon, now the Dragon Cart program) was made a program of record on April 1, 2026 with fielding targeted for 2027.

Lattice

Lattice is Anduril's software that fuses data from many sensors and ties its systems together. The video says Taiwan should buy it. Anduril has already said Lattice is integrated onto NCSIST-built systems, with a live-fire demonstration planned in 2025.

Copperhead

The video describes it as an underwater cruise missile that lies on the seabed and flies off the surface. The sources describe something different. Copperhead is a torpedo-type autonomous underwater vehicle in two sizes (100M and 500M), with a top speed of 30 knots. It is launched from surface vessels, aircraft, or larger undersea vehicles such as the Dive-LD and Dive-XL. The seabed role belongs to a separate product, the Seabed Sentry sensor network. NCSIST has said it plans to co-produce the Copperhead C-500M, and the number of platforms is classified.

Category 3 · Open questions

Roadrunner, Anvil, Pulsar, Fury, EagleEye

Roadrunner. A reusable twin-jet interceptor that is described as between a drone and a missile. I found no report that Taiwan has bought it, so the video's "rumor" stays unconfirmed. The video's cost-effectiveness argument is the speaker's opinion.

Anvil and Pulsar. The video uses "Pulsar" for two things. Pulsar is the electronic-warfare product. The quadcopter that rams other drones is Anvil. On Kuwait, the US State Department announced on June 5, 2026 a possible sale worth about $1.98 billion that includes Roadrunner-M, Anvil, Pulsar, Lattice, sentry towers and launch boxes. That is an approval, and quantities are not stated, so "Kuwait has already bought these" overstates it.

Fury (YFQ-44A). First flew on October 31, 2025, and Anduril started production at Arsenal-1 in March 2026. On a flight announced February 26, 2026 it was flown by both Shield AI's Hivemind and Anduril's Lattice Mission Autonomy in the same sortie, so "piloted by Hivemind" is only half the picture. The speaker's view that Taiwan would be better served by interceptors, and by a version that needs no runway, is opinion.

EagleEye. A helmet-mounted display system unveiled in late 2025, with a reported 84-degree field of view and Lattice data feeds. The Army approved moving Microsoft's IVAS contract to Anduril in April 2025. IVAS had a record of user complaints, and a July 2026 GAO assessment says none of the roughly 10,000 IVAS units will be fielded. Anduril received $159 million for the follow-on SBMC prototyping, and a competitor, Rivet, received $195 million.
